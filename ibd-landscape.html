A Closer Look
Evidence & Patient Landscape Map · Independent Work Sample

Beyond the two headline trials: the fuller CDED evidence base, where it sits in guideline care pathways, and who patients actually turn to.

A companion piece to my Evidence-to-Claim Brand Brief — mapping the wider public trial landscape around Modulen® and the Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet, where that evidence sits in published care pathways, the patient-advocacy organizations a launch would need market by market, and where global IBD epidemiology points for what comes after the UK and Canada.

This is an illustrative landscape map, not a systematic review and not a claim about Nestlé's internal evidence strategy. I searched PubMed-indexed journals, ClinicalTrials.gov-linked trial registrations (via sponsor documents and secondary listings, since clinicaltrials.gov itself blocks automated fetching), and each organization's own public materials. Where I couldn't confirm a specific number, I've said so rather than estimated. Nothing here has been reviewed by Nestlé Medical Affairs, and it's a snapshot as of August 2026, not a live-maintained database.

Work sample · Built August 2026 · Companion to the Evidence-to-Claim Brand Brief and ModuLife funnel teardown, prepared for the Global Marketing Lead, Inflammatory Bowel Diseases & Modulen® Brand Lead posting, Nestlé Nutrition & Health (Vevey).

The brand brief builds one claim from two trials. But a brand strategist translating "evidence... into relevant patient, customer and business insights" needs the full shape of the evidence base, not just its two strongest data points — including the trials that don't involve Modulen at all, since those are the competitive and guideline context the claim actually has to hold up against. This page is that fuller map, plus two things a claim alone doesn't cover: where this evidence actually sits in a real Crohn's care pathway, and which patient organizations a market-access or advocacy plan would need to engage.

So what Two findings matter more than the row count. First, the evidence base is still actively growing in exactly the direction a brand needs it to: from single-center pilots toward multi-country maintenance data (CD-HOPE, Sigall Boneh 2024) and a live EU adaptation trial (Sweden). Second, and less comfortable: TASTI-MM is a real, published, 2025 RCT showing a formula-free diet beating EEN on tolerability, with no Modulen or commercial formula required at all. That's not a reason to panic — CDED+PEN's own tolerability edge over EEN is arguably a bigger, better-replicated result — but it is a genuine, citable competitive signal that a "diet without a formula" pathway is gaining its own trial evidence, and a claim built only on today's numbers should account for it.
Where this sits in guidelines today The 2021 ECCO–ESPGHAN pediatric Crohn's guideline recommends exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) as first-line induction therapy (Level of Evidence 2, 92% panel agreement). It names CDED+PEN explicitly as a more tolerable emerging alternative with a higher proportion reaching sustained remission at week 12 in head-to-head testing — but states plainly that "replication studies, including data on mucosal healing, are required before strong recommendations can be made." In other words: CDED+Modulen is a real, guideline-acknowledged option today, not yet a guideline-mandated first-line one. That's the honest evidentiary position any HCP-facing claim should be built on.
Care Pathway

2. Where CDED+Modulen sits in the Crohn's patient journey

A simplified pathway from symptom onset to maintenance, built from the ECCO–ESPGHAN pediatric guideline and general UK/EU Crohn's management practice (NICE NG129) — not a substitute for either document.

1
Symptoms & primary care
Diarrhoea, pain, weight loss, fatigue prompt a GP visit; blood/faecal calprotectin testing.
2
GI referral & workup
Colonoscopy, biopsy, imaging confirm diagnosis and disease location/severity.
3
Induction decision
Steroids, EEN/CDED+PEN (esp. children, mild–moderate disease), or biologics for more severe disease.
4
Remission achieved
Symptom + biomarker (calprotectin) + ideally endoscopic/mucosal confirmation.
5
Maintenance
Biologics, immunomodulators, or emerging diet-based maintenance (CD-HOPE, Sigall Boneh 2024).
6
Monitoring & relapse
Ongoing calprotectin/endoscopy; relapse loops back to step 3. Surgery for structuring/fistulizing disease.
So what CDED+Modulen's strongest, most guideline-supported foothold is step 3 (induction, especially in children with mild-to-moderate disease) — exactly where the Levine 2019 evidence sits. Step 5 (maintenance) is where the newest evidence (CD-HOPE, Sigall Boneh 2024) is pointing next, but it's also the step furthest from a strong guideline recommendation today. A brand strategy that leads with maintenance claims would be running ahead of the evidence; one that owns induction hard and signals maintenance as "the next data readout" is the sequencing the evidence actually supports.

Middle East & Emerging Markets

4. The Middle East channel, and where epidemiology points next

On the Middle East hospital-channel question: I couldn't confirm it either way from public sources, and I'd rather say that plainly than guess. Modulen's Middle East product pages (nestlehealthscience-me.com, nestle-family.com) sit behind an "I am a healthcare professional" gate, which is consistent with institutional/clinical-channel distribution — but a gate is a marketing-page pattern, not proof of a bulk hospital-tender supply model. I found no public tender records, wholesale contracts, or pack-size data confirming bulk/institutional sales specifically. That's a real open question a Market Access briefing in this role would need to answer with internal data, not something I can responsibly claim from outside.

What I could confirm: the Gulf's patient-advocacy infrastructure is real, but young. The UAE IBD Society launched on World IBD Day, May 19, 2021 — founded by a practicing gastroenterologist (Dr. Maryam Al Khatry) under UAE Ministry of Community Development patronage. Saudi Arabia's equivalent is earlier-stage: public sources describe "steps towards" a Crohn's & Colitis Foundation of Saudi Arabia, built out of an IBD patient registry rather than an established foundation yet. Both are younger and less resourced than the five-market table in section 3 — a real signal for how a Gulf engagement plan would need to look (co-building awareness alongside a young organization, not distributing through an established one).

One number worth flagging, not trusting outright: a 2021 UAE news report on the IBD Society's launch cited "between two and four percent of the UAE population" as having IBD. That's roughly 5–10x even the highest published national IBD prevalence rates anywhere in the world (the Gastroenterology Advisor and Frontiers data below put Western Europe/North America, the current global ceiling, well under 1%). I'm including the figure because it's what the source says, not because I believe it — it reads like a media misstatement (possibly conflating IBD with IBS, or a much narrower sub-population), and I'd want it re-verified against an actual epidemiological study before it appeared in anything real.

On age groups and where global IBD incidence is actually accelerating: Kaplan's "four epidemiological stages" framework (Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2021) places Western nations — including all five in section 3's table — in "compounding prevalence," heading toward "prevalence equilibrium," with over 1% of their populations projected to live with IBD within a decade (2025 update to the same framework). Newly industrializing regions — much of Asia, Latin America, and parts of the Middle East/North Africa — sit further back, in "acceleration in incidence," projected to shift toward compounding prevalence over the next two decades, not overnight.

A 2025 global-burden analysis of pediatric/adolescent IBD (1990–2021) sharpens that regional picture with real numbers: East Asia is the fastest-accelerating region specifically for pediatric-onset IBD (+2.01%/year), while North America is now declining (−1.07%/year) as its pediatric incidence plateaus. By age band, 15–19-year-olds account for 42.6% of incident pediatric/adolescent cases globally — the single largest group, and one that overlaps closely with the Levine 2019 trial population itself (ages 4–18, mean 14.2).

So what These aren't the same kind of gap as the UK and Canada in section 3 — those have the regulatory pathway, the reimbursement precedent, and the formula already on pharmacy shelves; the missing piece is specifically the branded program. Brazil, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia are a step behind that: the formula and a young advocacy partner already exist, but the epidemiological case is still building, not built. East Asia is a step behind again — the single strongest epidemiological signal in this whole page, and I found no Modulen presence and no confirmed patient-org partner to build on, which makes it a real research question for this role, not a launch candidate yet. Mexico is here as a reminder to check the country, not the continent, before calling anywhere "emerging."
