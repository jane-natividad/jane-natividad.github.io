"Global brand experience" is a line almost every candidate for this role will write on a CV. The harder, rarer thing underneath that phrase is knowing that global isn't one strategy copied 9 times — it's 9 different regulatory systems, reimbursement structures, and patient-advocacy landscapes that each fail in their own way. The 3 pieces below are the evidence for that instinct: a real 9-market footprint, a launch sequence where the bottleneck changes market to market, and an epidemiology-led view of which markets are worth the sequence next.

The Footprint

1. 9 markets live. 2 notable gaps — in the markets you'd least expect.

ModuLife®, the branded CDED program built around Modulen®, has already proven it can travel: a real, repeatable localization playbook with its own registered-provider directory in each market, not a pilot. Checking Nestlé's own public brand pages market by market turns up 9 live storefronts — and 2 absences that are more interesting than they'd first appear, because they're not hard-to-reach emerging markets. They're 2 of Modulen's most regulatorily mature markets, with NHS and Health Canada prescribing infrastructure already in place.

So what My hypothesis, clearly flagged as a guess: both UK and Canada sit outside the EU FSMP framework the other 9 markets share (UK post-Brexit, Canada under a different food-law system entirely), so the templated EU rollout doesn't port over automatically. 9 successful launches don't predict the tenth and eleventh — that's a sequencing decision, and it's exactly the kind of multi-market call this role is built to make.
The Launch Sequence

2. 3 markets, 3 different failure modes

Closing the UK and Canada gaps — and deepening the program where it already exists in Germany — runs through the same 4 functions every time: Evidence, Regulatory, Market Access, Brand/Sales. What changes market to market is which one is actually the bottleneck.

So what None of these 3 markets fail the same way. A global plan that treats "UK," "Germany," and "Canada" as the same kind of problem stalls in at least one of them — which is the whole argument for a role that sits above all 3 functions instead of inside just one.
What Comes After

3. Where the epidemiology says to look next — and where it says not to, yet

Closing UK and Canada isn't the end of the map. Global IBD incidence is accelerating unevenly, and the honest way to read "next markets" is against that epidemiology, not against which markets feel familiar.

So what These aren't the UK/Canada kind of gap — those have the regulatory pathway and the formula already on pharmacy shelves, and the missing piece is just the branded program. Brazil, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia are a step behind that: the formula and a young advocacy partner already exist, but the epidemiological case is still building. East Asia is a step behind again — the strongest epidemiological signal on this page, with no Modulen presence and no confirmed patient-org partner to build on yet, which makes it a research question for this role, not a launch candidate today. Mexico is here as a reminder to check the country, not the continent, before calling anywhere "emerging."
The thesis

Global doesn't mean one playbook — it means knowing which of 9 markets need the same playbook and which 3 need their own.
