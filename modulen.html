A Closer Look
Evidence-to-Claim Brand Brief · Independent Work Sample

One RCT, two audiences, three markets: turning the CDED trial data into a Modulen® claim that would actually clear Regulatory.

Nestlé already built ModuLife® to commercialize this evidence — but its own brand pages show it live in only two of the markets where Modulen® itself is sold. Here's the claim architecture and a launch sequence for closing that gap in three more.

This is an illustrative work sample, not a claim about Nestlé's internal process or confidential data. Every clinical figure, regulatory citation, and market-availability statement below comes from published, publicly available sources — the trial papers themselves, EU/UK/Germany/Canada regulatory text, and Nestlé's own public brand and prescriber-facing pages (listed in Sources). Where public information was incomplete — for example, whether ModuLife® is actively marketed beyond the markets where I could find a live brand page — I've said so rather than guessed. Nothing here has been reviewed by Nestlé Regulatory, Legal, or Medical Affairs, and it isn't intended to be used as-is.

Work sample · Built August 2026 · Prepared for the Global Marketing Lead, Inflammatory Bowel Diseases & Modulen® Brand Lead posting, Nestlé Nutrition & Health (Vevey).

The posting asks for someone who can translate "evidence, medical topics and claims into relevant patient, customer and business insights" — and apply "regulatory frameworks, healthcare systems, market access, claims development" to get there compliantly. Rather than describe that skill, I applied it to a real, public evidence base: the randomized trials behind the Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet (CDED) combined with Modulen®. What follows is the claim that evidence supports, why it has to be written two different ways for two different audiences, where Nestlé's own ModuLife® program has and hasn't reached yet, and a launch sequence for closing that gap in the UK, Germany, and Canada.

The Evidence

1. What the trials actually show

Two randomized controlled trials anchor the CDED+Modulen evidence base — one pediatric, one adult, both investigator-led and independently published.

Levine et al., Gastroenterology 2019 — multicentre RCT, 78 randomized (74 analyzed), ages 4–18, mild-to-moderate luminal Crohn's disease (PCDAI 10–40), 10 sites in Israel + 2 in Canada. CDED + partial enteral nutrition (PEN, using Modulen) vs. exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) alone.
  • Week 6 remission (PCDAI <10, steroid-free): 75% (CDED+PEN) vs. 59% (EEN) — not significant (p=0.38).
  • Week 12 sustained remission: 75.6% (28/37) vs. 45.1% (14/31) — p=0.01.
  • Tolerability: 97.5% vs. 73.7% completion (OR 13.92, 95% CI 1.68–115.14, p=0.002).
Yanai et al., Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology 2022 — open-label pilot RCT, 3 sites in Israel, 40 biologic-naïve adults (18–55) with mild-to-moderate disease (Harvey-Bradshaw Index 5–14), CDED+PEN vs. CDED alone.
  • Week 6 remission: 68% vs. 57% (not significant, p=0.46) — CDED works with or without the formula at induction.
  • Week 24: 80% of week-6 responders sustained remission; 35% of all patients reached endoscopic remission.
So what The honest read of this evidence isn't "Modulen cures Crohn's" — it's that CDED, using Modulen to deliver the formula-food ratio the diet requires, sustains remission significantly better than exclusive enteral nutrition in children (p=0.01) and is tolerated dramatically better (p=0.002), with early but real signal on endoscopic healing in adults. That's a strong, specific, defensible claim — if it's written correctly for who's reading it.
Claim Architecture

2. One evidence base, two compliant claims

In the EU, Modulen is a Food for Special Medical Purposes (FSMP) under Regulation (EU) No 609/2013 and Delegated Regulation 2016/128 — and under Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006, nutrition and health claims may not be made on an FSMP. The label can (and must) state what the product is for — "dietary management of Crohn's disease" — but it can't carry a promotional efficacy claim like "75.6% sustained remission." That evidence has a legitimate home; it's just not on the pack.

HCP-directed scientific claim

"In a randomized controlled trial (Levine et al., Gastroenterology 2019), CDED combined with partial enteral nutrition using Modulen achieved sustained, corticosteroid-free remission at 12 weeks in 75.6% of pediatric patients vs. 45.1% with exclusive enteral nutrition (p=0.01), with significantly higher treatment completion (97.5% vs. 73.7%, p=0.002)."

Channel: Medical Affairs scientific exchange — congress materials, medical information responses, peer-reviewed publication dissemination. Not an advertising claim; addressed to a healthcare professional, sourced directly to the published trial.
Patient / caregiver value proposition

"The Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet, delivered through the ModuLife® program, pairs a structured food plan with Modulen® — for the dietary management of Crohn's disease, under your care team's supervision — so managing the disease doesn't mean giving up eating."

Channel: FSMP-compliant labelling / patient program materials. States purpose and mechanism, not a bare efficacy statistic — matching how Modulen's own UK, Canada, and US labels are actually written today.
Why this split matters Getting this wrong in either direction is a real failure mode: writing the patient-facing line like the HCP line risks an unlawful health claim on an FSMP; writing the HCP line as vaguely as the patient line wastes the trial's actual statistical strength with the one audience licensed to hear it in full. This read of EU claims law is based on public EUR-Lex text, not a substitute for Nestlé Regulatory sign-off.
Market Footprint

3. Where Modulen® and ModuLife® stand today

Modulen (the formula) and ModuLife (the branded CDED program built around it) are not the same launch. Checking Nestlé's own public brand and prescriber pages market by market shows the formula travels much further than the program does.

So what ModuLife is the vehicle that turns this trial evidence into an actual patient- and HCP-facing offering, not just a formula on a shelf — and right now, three of Modulen's five public markets don't have it. That's not a knock on the brand; a US/Middle-East-first sequencing is a defensible way to prove the model before scaling it into markets with harder reimbursement gates. It's the next, obvious brand-strategy move — and it's exactly the kind of multi-market sequencing call this role is built to make.
Launch Sequence

4. Bringing ModuLife® to the UK, Germany & Canada

Four phases, each with one function clearly in the lead — because "influencing without direct authority" only works if everyone knows whose call it is at each step.

1
Evidence Package
Medical Affairs assembles the Levine/Yanai data plus any local real-world evidence into a market-ready scientific narrative.
2
Regulatory & Claims Clearance
Regulatory confirms the HCP-vs-patient claim split holds locally and clears program materials against national FSMP/advertising law.
3
Market Access Foundations
Market Access secures the local reimbursement or prescribing pathway before any HCP-facing launch activity goes live.
4
Coordinated Launch
Brand + Sales activate HCPs and caregivers together, once evidence, claims, and access are all cleared — not before.

What changes market to market is which phase is actually the bottleneck:

MarketRegulatory's jobMarket Access's jobMedical Affairs' job

GermanyClear program materials under GKV advertising rules for prescription-only nutritionBuild the "medizinisch notwendig" (medically necessary) documentation case with G-BA/SHI criteria for a disease-specific formula, since it doesn't get the standard-formula fast laneGenerate the local health-economic and clinical narrative Market Access needs for that case

So what None of these three markets fail the same way. UK risk is scope (does the existing approval stretch to a program?); Germany risk is documentation (can Market Access build the medical-necessity case a specialised formula needs?); Canada risk is fragmentation (there's no single reimbursement decision to win). A global launch plan that treats all three identically will stall in at least one of them — which is the whole argument for a role that sits above all three functions instead of inside just one.
