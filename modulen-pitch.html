What I'd Validate First

6 questions this research surfaced

Each one unfolds into what I found from public sources alone — and what I'd confirm first, before assuming I've got it right.

Market Access Modulen's been prescribed in the UK and Canada for years. Why isn't ModuLife there? View SolutionHide

9 other markets already have a live, localized ModuLife program — a repeatable playbook, on paper. The UK and Canada don't, despite the formula itself being NHS- and Health Canada-prescribable for years.

My read: both sit outside the EU FSMP framework the other nine markets share, so the standard rollout doesn't port over automatically. UK's fix looks like a scope question — extending an approval built for a formula to a program. Canada's looks like fragmentation — no single reimbursement gate, just insurers and provinces one by one.

Digital Funnel The real customer isn't the patient — it's the clinician who grants them access. Is the funnel built that way? View SolutionHide

ModuLife is B2B2C: a patient can't self-enroll, only a registered clinician can grant app access. But the clinician's actual sign-up path routes to a bare login screen — no "why," no time estimate — right next to a marketing page that sells the program hard.

That handoff gap is a specific, fixable leak in the one funnel that actually drives growth here — not a redesign, a rewrite of one page.

Global Footprint Nine markets are live. Which ones are actually working, and which just launched? View SolutionHide

All nine have a localized site and a real, verifiable provider network — individually named clinicians, not a shell. But "live" isn't the same as "working."

Germany's program is active, yet the formula itself still lacks the fast-lane reimbursement status a standard formula gets — meaning program access and full market access are two different milestones, not one.

Claims & Evidence The evidence supports a strong claim — but only for one audience. Which one, and why? View SolutionHide

The strongest number in the whole evidence base — 75.6% vs 45.1% sustained remission, p=0.01 — is real, published, and usable with HCPs in scientific exchange.

On an FSMP label or in patient-facing materials, that exact language would likely need Regulatory/Legal sign-off before use — it reads as a health claim outside HCP scientific exchange. Getting the HCP-vs-patient split right is the difference between using the evidence and burying it.

Epidemiology Where is IBD growing fastest — and does Modulen have a presence there yet? View SolutionHide

East Asia has the fastest-rising pediatric IBD incidence of any region tracked (+2.01%/yr) — and no Modulen presence or confirmed advocacy partner.

Brazil, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia are the opposite profile: the formula is already live and a young advocacy partner exists to build with, but the epidemiological case is still developing. Different markets, different kinds of "not ready yet."

Competitive Signal A 2025 trial shows a formula-free diet beating EEN on tolerability. Does that change the pitch? View SolutionHide

TASTI-MM is real, published, peer-reviewed evidence that a diet without any commercial formula can outperform EEN on tolerability.

CDED+PEN's own tolerability edge is arguably stronger and better-replicated — but a brand strategy that ignores this signal is planning against yesterday's competitive landscape, not today's.
