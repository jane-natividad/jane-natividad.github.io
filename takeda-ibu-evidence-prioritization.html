Evidence Governance Framework · Independent Work

An IBU evidence prioritization framework, built before applying, for a business unit that's four months old.

A governance and weighted-scoring model for consolidating local evidence needs across 50+ IBU markets into one ranked voice feeding the global Integrated Evidence Generation Plan (IEGP).

This is an illustrative model, not a claim about Takeda's internal process. Takeda's International Business Unit (IBU) was only consolidated as a business unit on April 1, 2026, so there is very likely no established evidence-governance framework to describe, only one to build. Everything below is built on public therapeutic-area and market information; it contains no confidential, proprietary, or real internal Takeda data. It's an example of how I'd approach designing this governance model, not a description of what Takeda is already doing.

Work sample · Built August 2026 · Illustrative governance and scoring framework, prepared for the Evidence Generation and Dissemination Lead, IBU posting (R0182858).

The role's first listed responsibility is to "design and own the IBU prioritization framework for evidence generation needs across therapeutic areas." Rather than describe how I'd approach that in a cover letter, I built the framework: a governance flow for how local evidence requests reach one ranked IBU list, a weighted scoring rubric, and a worked example applying both to six illustrative evidence priorities across five therapeutic areas.

Why this exists Right now, each IBU country and therapeutic area team most likely asks for evidence separately, with no shared way to decide what's worth doing, what's redundant, and what should be escalated to global. This page is what turning that scattered input into one coherent, defensible IBU voice actually looks like when I do it, not a general statement of capability, but a specific governance model and a scoring method a Prioritization Council could use on day one.
Governance Model

1. Local input to global representation

Five steps carry a request from a country team's intake form to a seat at the table in global evidence generation planning.

1
Local Country Teams
50+ EUCAN & GEM markets submit evidence needs via a standardized structured intake tool (need, population, urgency, existing data).
2
TA Lead Consolidation
Therapeutic Area Leads (Onc, GI, Neuro, Rare Disease, PDT) de-duplicate and cluster requests within their TA.
3
IBU Prioritization Council
Quarterly cross-TA review scores and ranks consolidated needs using the weighted rubric below.
4
IBU Ranked Evidence Plan
One ranked list of IBU-wide evidence and dissemination priorities, with rationale, owner, and recommended vehicle.
5
Global IEGP Representation
IBU Lead represents the ranked plan in the global evidence generation and publication forum; negotiates resourcing and alignment.
Feedback loop (continuous): every request receives an explicit status, approved, deferred, or declined, with rationale routed back to the originating country team. That's what closes the loop and builds trust in the process, especially for a role with no formal authority over the teams submitting requests.
Scoring Rubric

2. A weighted rubric, not a gut call

Each criterion is scored 1–5 by the Prioritization Council; the weighted composite (max 5.0) ranks every consolidated request across therapeutic areas on one comparable scale.

CriterionWt.What it captures
Unmet evidence need25%Size of the gap between evidence that exists and what's needed for a market/clinical decision.
Market & patient impact20%Number of IBU markets and patients affected if the evidence is generated.
HTA / payer urgency20%Time-sensitivity relative to upcoming HTA reviews, reassessments, or reimbursement decisions.
Cross-market reusability15%Whether one asset (study, dossier, registry) can serve multiple markets vs. one-off local use.
Feasibility10%Data availability, cost, and realistic time to execute.
Strategic / IEGP alignment10%Fit with global brand strategy and existing IEGP priorities.

So what for Takeda This is the artifact a Prioritization Council could start using in its first quarterly review, not a concept to workshop from scratch. The rubric already reflects the JD's five critical capabilities directly: unmet need and market impact map to strategic prioritization, the governance flow above is the governance design, the Council structure is matrix influence without direct authority, IEGP alignment is the enterprise perspective, and cross-market reusability is cross-market collaboration.
Try the working version

This page is the Takeda-specific application of a weighted-scoring engine I built and keep as a general-purpose, publicly reusable tool, not branded to any one employer. The live version recomputes every ranking instantly as you adjust the six criteria weights: fully deterministic and inspectable, no model in the scoring loop, so every position traces back to the underlying scores and weights. AI is used only to structure messy free-text intake into a comparable record; the ranking itself stays human-auditable, which is the credibility a governance role like this one needs.
