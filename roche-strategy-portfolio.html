Portfolio Prioritization — Work Sample

Where should early-stage R&D place its next bet?

Jane Natividad, PhD  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  linkedin.com/in/jane-natividad

Every early-R&D leadership team is running some version of this call right now: given finite discovery capacity, how much incremental investment goes toward a hot, fast-moving therapeutic area versus doubling down on the disease areas you already have platform depth in? There usually isn't a single right answer — there's a right answer for a given set of priorities. This tool doesn't pick a winner. It makes the trade-off explicit, so the argument moves from "my program vs. yours" to "here's what we're actually optimizing for."
Set the priorities
Resulting ranking
Unmet need
— how far current standard of care falls short.
Tractability
— probability of technical success given today's biology and modality maturity.
Differentiation
— competitive white space rather than a crowded field.
Platform fit
— how much existing capability, translational infrastructure, and internal expertise this leverages.
Speed to proof-of-concept
— how quickly a bet can be de-risked or killed.
"The scores behind each bet are fixed — what moves is how much weight leadership puts on each criterion. Slide 'unmet need' to the top and neuroscience wins even though it's slower and harder. Slide 'speed to proof-of-concept' up and the calculus flips. Neither is wrong. The job isn't finding the answer, it's getting research, market access, and finance to agree on the weights before the debate starts — so the ranking is trusted, not argued with."
Illustrative work sample, not a Roche or third-party confidential document. The four therapeutic bets reflect publicly known industry dynamics (including Roche's own public Carmot Therapeutics acquisition in the metabolic space); the underlying 0–10 scores are directional judgment calls made for demonstration purposes, not internal pRED data.
