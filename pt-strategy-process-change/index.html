Strategic Process & Change Management — Work Sample

A GPS for organizational change: one rollout, tracked from impact to adoption

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  linkedin.com/in/jane-natividad

Deciding what a process change should be is the easy half. The hard half is what this page is about: knowing who's affected, telling each of them what actually changes for them, using AI to draft that translation work without letting it publish anything on its own, and watching — honestly — whether adoption is real or just reported. One fictional change, two points of view: the change lead running it, and one stakeholder group living through it.

Governance, by design not by policy doc
1
Human approval gateEvery AI-drafted brief sits in a visibly "awaiting review" state until the change lead approves it. Nothing publishes to a stakeholder group on its own.
2
ProvenanceEvery drafted brief is generated directly from the structured change record above — the same fields are visible to you as fed the draft, nothing is hidden inside a prompt.
3
Editable, not just approvableA change lead can edit any draft before approving it — the AI's first pass is a starting point, not a final answer.
4
No autonomous escalationWhen adoption signals suggest intervention, the page surfaces a recommended next step and a flag — it never marks anything "resolved" or "escalated" by itself.
5
Synthetic data onlyThe change, the three sites, the five stakeholder groups, and every adoption signal on this page are fictional, built for this demonstration. No real Roche process, site, employee, or metric is represented anywhere.
What this deliberately does not do
  • No real organization is modeled — three sites and five stakeholder groups stand in for the shape of a large matrixed rollout, not an attempt to simulate one at real scale.
  • No model call, no API key, no network request — the "AI drafts" on this page are deterministic templates built from the structured fields you can see, not a live language-model call. That's a deliberate choice for this piece (a live, grounded call is demonstrated on the RAG work sample instead), not a limitation of the pattern.
  • No autonomous decisions — the page never approves its own drafts, never marks a rollout "adopted," and never resolves an escalation. Every judgment call sits with a human throughout.
  • No persistence — approvals and edits live in this browser tab's memory only and reset on refresh; nothing you do here is sent or saved anywhere.
"The interesting design problem in enterprise change isn't the announcement — it's the translation. What does this actually mean for a supervisor on one shift at one site, versus a planner two functions away? AI is genuinely useful for drafting that translation at scale. It is not useful, and shouldn't be trusted, to decide on its own that the translation is right, or that adoption is happening. That boundary is what this page tries to make visible rather than just claim."
